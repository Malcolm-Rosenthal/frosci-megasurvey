# Video brief: Great Molasses Flood reel (Sandwich Team, Wed 12:10)

The video is the "short-form" treatment. The other group reads the same 10 facts as a plain list for
exactly as long as the video lasts, so **the narration must contain these facts and nothing else**.

## Specs

- **Vertical 9:16** (1080×1920 or 720×1280), **45–60 seconds**.
- **Captions burned in** (big word-by-word captions, the usual reel style). Many people will watch without sound.
- AI voiceover reading the script below **exactly**. The name "Great Molasses Flood" must be said (the quiz uses it). Small changes to rhythm are fine; no added facts, numbers or names.
- Visuals: anything reel-like (AI-generated or stock clips of old Boston, a harbor, a giant tank, sticky brown goo…).
  **On-screen text other than the captions shouldn't add facts** (no extra dates, numbers or names).
- No death or injury imagery.
- Background music is fine.
- Export as **MP4 (H.264)**, **under 20 MB**. Save as `studies/sandwich-1210/media/molasses-reel.mp4`
  (replacing the placeholder). The fact list's on-screen time adjusts to the video's length automatically.

## Script (≈120 words)

> The Great Molasses Flood: the weirdest disaster you've never heard of.
> January 15, 1919.
> Boston's North End.
> It's an unusually warm winter day — over 40 degrees.
> Then a giant tank holding 2.3 million gallons of molasses bursts open.
> That molasses was supposed to become alcohol for making munitions.
> And the tank? It leaked so badly, the company painted it brown to hide the leaks.
> The wave of molasses rushed through the streets at 35 miles per hour.
> The company's excuse? They blamed anarchists for blowing up the tank.
> Boston Harbor stayed brown with molasses until that summer.
> In the end, the company paid $628,000 in damages.
> Follow for more history they never taught you.

## How to make it

"Faceless reel" generators take a script and produce the voiceover, visuals and captions in one go.
CapCut and InVideo AI are common options; most have a free tier that adds a watermark (fine for this). Paste the script, choose a
vertical format, a voice and caption style, then check that the voice reads it word for word before exporting.
